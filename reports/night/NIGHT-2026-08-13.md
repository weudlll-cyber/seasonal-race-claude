## PIECE 5 — the TrackEditor.loadmode timeout — **the budget is fine; the concurrency is not**

**I did not raise the timeout, and it does not need raising.**

| | suite wall | loadmode file | worst test in it | files running concurrently |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| the file alone | — | 10.3 s | **481 ms** | 1 |
| **full suite, default (13 workers)** | **136.2 s** | 5682 ms | **1491 ms** | **14** |
| **full suite, `--maxWorkers=6`** | **136.4 s** | 2544 ms | **670 ms** | **4** |

**The finding: `goldenRealArm.test.js` is 99% of the suite's wall clock** — 134.7 s of 136.2 s. The
suite is not throughput-bound, it is bound by **one file**. So running 13 workers instead of 6 cannot
make the suite finish sooner, and **measured, it does not: 136.2 s versus 136.4 s, a difference of
0.2 s.** What the extra workers *do* buy is contention, and the tests that pay for it are the
latency-sensitive React/jsdom ones whose `waitFor` polls stretch under CPU starvation.

**Two hypotheses I tested and discarded before landing on that:**

- *"It is the four long parity files hogging cores."* **No.** Running loadmode alongside only those
  four gives a worst test of **454 ms** — indistinguishable from running it alone. Four busy cores out
  of fourteen cost nothing. It is the *number* of concurrent files, not those files.
- *"It is the 5 s budget being too tight for a suite this size."* **No.** At six workers the worst
  test is 670 ms, which is **13% of the budget**. There is nothing marginal about it once the
  contention is gone.

**Corroborating number:** the sum of all per-file wall times falls from **535.8 s to 216.4 s** at six
workers — a 2.5× reduction in total CPU-seconds for the same work. That difference *is* the
contention overhead, measured directly.

**The fix, with its cost: cap the fork pool, and the cost is 0.2 s of wall clock.** One line in
`client/vitest.config.js`. **I did not ship it** — it changes the machine every future measurement in
this project runs on, and that is your call, not a decision to make unattended at 2am. See PROPOSAL 1.

**Not fixed, and worth knowing:** the parity tests set their own per-test budget explicitly
(`RACE_TIMEOUT_MS` as `it`'s third argument). That is the right pattern and it is already in use — so
if a test ever genuinely needs more than 5 s, the project already knows how to say so without moving
the global default.

## PROPOSALS

### 1. Cap the vitest fork pool at 6 — 0.2 s of wall clock for a 2.2× quieter suite

The measurement is in Piece 5 and it is unusually clean: the suite's wall clock is set by one file
(99% of it), so the extra workers buy literally nothing in time and cost every latency-sensitive test
a factor of 2–3. **One line in `client/vitest.config.js`.** The reason this matters beyond tidiness:
a suite whose per-test durations swing 3× with load cannot be used to notice a real performance
regression, and this project already had `retry: 3` hiding exactly that class of failure until
ONE-TRUTH-2 removed it.

### 2. Split `goldenRealArm.test.js`, because it IS the suite's wall clock

134.7 s of a 136.2 s run, in **four tests**. Every other file finishes inside it. Splitting it by
seed into four files would let the pool overlap them and would likely take the whole suite under a
minute — the single largest inner-loop win available, and bigger than anything in Proposal 1.
`VERIFY-RULES.md` currently names `goldenEquality.test.js` as the file to look at "if the inner loop
needs to get faster"; **that is now the wrong file, and this report is the evidence.** It is 73 s
against goldenRealArm's 135 s.

### 3. Stamp the ending's measured numbers, now that Piece 4 makes that real

`docs/ENDING-PHASES.md` phase 6 carries "~2.9 s at 20 racers" and "the zoom-out starts ~1.4 s before
it ends", both unstamped, and item 10 above shows at least one of them is contradicted by an earlier
measurement. Until tonight a stamp there would have been decoration. It no longer is. This is the
single highest-value stamp available, because the ending is the part of the game that changed most in
the last two days.

### 4. Ask the longitudinal question directly

Piece 2 establishes that the ending's framing is bounded by a *width* rule that works for *length*
reasons. That is a real design smell — the bound that fires is not the bound that is doing the work.
A bound stated on the field's longitudinal extent would be honest about what it is for, could be
tuned without touching the width, and is the only route to the narrower ending you asked for. It is a
block, not an aside.
