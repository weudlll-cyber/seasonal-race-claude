## THE ANSWER IN SIX SENTENCES

1. **The quantity in the backlog item — "pan displacement" — is the wrong one, and that is settled
   rather than argued.** `AIM-ROOM-PAN-ANATOMY-1` withdrew it on 2026-09-02 and `RACE-JUDDER-1`
   derived the same correction independently: `Δ(offsetX, offsetY)` is the bookkeeping a
   zoom-about-the-origin forces on the offset, not the picture.
2. **With the right quantity — the camera CENTRE in world px, in frame widths — the shipped tree is
   quiet**: the picture never moves more than **7.0% of a frame width** in one frame in either arm.
3. **A multiple of the local median works for pan and is UNDEFINED for zoom**: the zoom median is
   exactly **0.00000** on 9 of 10 headless tracks and on both browser races, so the zoom channel must
   be an absolute `|Δ ln|`.

   the browser race it is **the largest picture-pan of the whole race (0.0700 frame widths, ×14.8 the
   local median) AND the largest zoom step (0.1276 ln)**, on a frame with **no state change, no
   lerp-phase change and no HUD change**. Every bound worth setting sits within a few percent of it,

## 1 · WHICH QUANTITY

### 1.1 What the records already contain

`scripts/camera-fingerprint.mjs:274–285` hashes exactly:

```
cd.state | cd.lerpPhase | cd.anchorRacerLabel | zoom | offsetX | offsetY
         | targetZoom | targetOffsetX | targetOffsetY | camT
```

Everything a step check needs to **compute** is there. Almost nothing it needs to **exempt** is,
beyond `state` and `lerpPhase`. And it is a **hash** — the rows are consumed into a digest and never
retained. A hash answers "did it change", never "is it discontinuous". That is the backlog item's
complaint, stated precisely.

The **browser dump** is richer. `client/src/modules/viewerProbe.js:406–419` writes a 45-key per-frame
row including `ox, oy, ez, st, b, lerp, anchorSX/SY, lagX/lagY, zoomRatio`, where **`b` is
`_framingProbe.binding`**, fed from `client/src/screens/RaceScreen/index.jsx:1613`. It carries a
width-authority label per frame. It does **not** carry the retirement latch (§3.6).

### 1.2 The quantity is NOT Δoffset — withdrawn, twice

`reports/evolution/AIM-ROOM-PAN-ANATOMY-1.md:17–24`:

> `camStep` is `hypot(offsetX − prevOffsetX, offsetY − prevOffsetY)`, and `projection.toScreen` is
> `pt.x * camZoom * axisX + offsetX` … **`offsetX`/`offsetY` and therefore `camStep` are SCREEN
> PIXELS.** … **But the offset is not the picture.** The camera zooms about the world origin, so when
> the zoom changes the offset must move to keep the subject framed. **A large `camStep` accompanied by
> a zoom change says nothing on its own about apparent motion.**

`reports/evolution/RACE-JUDDER-1.md:56–61`, from the other direction:

> A world point at screen `X` moves by `Δoffset + (X − offset) × (Δzoom / zoom)`. **Pan and zoom
> largely cancel** … **On the frame with the largest raw pan step, the offset moved 742 px and the
> worst point on the canvas moved 53 px. Fourteen times smaller.**

**I reproduced that 742 px frame exactly** (`ss9.json` frame 4898, `dOff = 742 px`) — which confirms
my reader is reading the same thing that report read — and my centre-based measure puts it at
**0.0070 frame widths, ×1.0 the local median.** The centre barely moved; the canvas *edge* moved 53
px. Both are true and they answer different questions (§1.3).

**Ranking check, headless arm.** Maximum single-frame `hypot(Δoffset)` per track: 449.9 / 456.9 /
511.6 / 512.1 / 538.4 / 621.4 / 704.4 / 1207.9 / 1377.5 / 2332.4 px. Maximum single-frame *picture*
pan, same races: 0.0221 … 0.0533 frame widths. **The two rankings are not the same ranking.**
mountainstreet has the second-largest offset step (1377.5 px) and the *smallest* picture step
(0.0221 max; 0.0065 on that very frame).

### 1.3 The quantity to use

Two channels, because pan and zoom are not commensurable and merging them hides which moved —
`scripts/finish-motion-truth.mjs:12–13` already says exactly this.

- **PAN — the camera centre in WORLD px, divided by the visible world width.** No new field needed:
  `cx = (CW/2 − offsetX) / (zoom · axisX)`, `visW = CW / (zoom · axisX)`; `axisX/axisY` from
  `projection.js:97–105`. Unit: **frame widths** — the perceptual unit, because half a frame looks the
  same whether the frame is 400 or 4,000 world px wide.
- **ZOOM — `|Δ ln camZoom|`.** Log space, because scale is perceived logarithmically; the project
  already reasons this way (`CameraDirector.js:4726`, *"THE BLEND IS IN LOG SPACE"*).

**Both are already implemented in the tree, and neither is a check.**
`scripts/diag/runin-camera-motion.mjs:10–14` defines series (a) as *"how far the view centre travels
between two frames, in world px AND as a fraction of the visible frame width. The fraction is the
perceptual unit"*. `scripts/diag/runin-anatomy.mjs:226` and `scripts/diag/runin-aim-axes.mjs:216–219`
compute `camCentre` identically. `scripts/diag/judder-census.mjs:27–34` computes the *unified* form —
the worst on-canvas point, `dX = Δoffset + (X − offset_prev)·(Δeff/eff_prev)`, at both X edges.
**Every one of them is report-only and scoped to one strand.**

**Recommendation on form: keep two channels; report `worstX` beside them, do not gate on it.** The
unified number is the better perceptual measure, but it merges a fault and a deliberate zoom cut into
one scalar and then cannot exempt them separately — which is the problem this piece exists to solve.

**One live false comment to fix while nearby.** `scripts/diag/leader-lag-tc.mjs:9` says *"`camStep` is
how far a fixed world point slides per frame"*. True only at constant zoom. Eight sites use `camStep`
(`aim-levers.mjs:160`, `headcount-price.mjs:125`, `leader-lag-truth.mjs:222`,
`leader-lateral-minimal.mjs:219`, plus four `*-sum` readers).

### 2.2 The threshold shapes, and why they differ

**ZOOM cannot take the brief's form.** Its median is exactly zero on 11 of 12 races — the zoom sits
still on most frames, by design. A multiple of the local median divides by zero and reports every
non-zero step as infinitely anomalous: at a nominal ×50 that is **96–240 frames per track** in the
headless arm. Not a tunable threshold — a broken one. **Ruled out by measurement.** It must be an
absolute `|Δ ln|`.

**PAN takes it, with a second condition and a short window.**

- **Window: ±30 frames, excluding the frame itself** (one second of context each side). It must be
  short: a whole-race median cannot separate a glide from a follow, and a whole-race *maximum* is
  unusable because `COMPANY-HEADCOUNT-1.md:130–137` records healthy master carrying a **3,863 px**
  offset maximum on seatrack **in both arms**.
- **AND `dPanFrac > 0.005`.** Without it a frame that moves 0.4 px against a 0.02 px median is a
  ratio of 20 and a picture that did not move.

**Proposed bounds, with their measured cost on the tree as it stands:**

| channel | bound | hits, browser arm | hits, headless arm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PAN | ≥ **15×** local median AND > 0.005 frame widths | **0** | **0** |
| PAN | ≥ **10×** local median AND > 0.005 frame widths | **2** — both the field retirement | **1** in 43,022 |
| ZOOM | `\|Δ ln\| ≥ **0.25**` (×1.28 in one frame) | 0 | 0 |
| ZOOM | `\|Δ ln\| ≥ **0.15**` | **1** — the field retirement | 6 — all field retirements |

**Read the two ×10/0.15 rows together and the design decides itself.** Every bound tight enough to
catch this week's real events is a bound the field-guarantee retirement trips or nearly trips; every
bound loose enough to spare it is loose enough to miss them (§4). **The retirement is not noise near
the threshold — it is, on most races, the single largest event in both channels.** That is why the
marker, not the number, is the decision.

### 2.3 Does it catch the incident it was proposed for?

`FINISH-MOTION-1.md` records that at the FINISH\_OVERVIEW entry *"the director's own probe shows the
resolved camera position moving **396.7 world px** while the anchor it frames moved 6.5"*. I measured

Against dirt-oval's whole-race maximum of **0.0417** and its local median of **0.00676**, that is

211 px and whose maximum on another track is 2,332 px: **the wrong quantity puts the fault inside the
tree-wide noise; the right one puts it two orders of magnitude outside.**

### 3.9 THE GUARD THAT ALREADY EXISTS, AND WHAT IS WRONG WITH IT

`scripts/viewer-invariants.mjs:54` already declares that it covers *"no frame changing the picture
beyond a stated bound"*, implemented at `client/src/modules/viewerProbe.js:319–334`:

```js
const dLn = Math.abs(Math.log(width / _prev.width));
if (dLn > STEP_LN_MAX)  add('4-widthstep', …)          // STEP_LN_MAX = Math.log(2)  (:97)
const dPan = Math.hypot(f.offsetX - _prev.offsetX, f.offsetY - _prev.offsetY);
if (dPan >= CW)         add('4-panstep',  `the picture moved ${…} px sideways in one frame …`)
```

Two defects, both measurable:

1. **`4-panstep` grades the withdrawn quantity, and its event text is false as English.** It reports

## 6 · WHAT I COULD NOT ESTABLISH

- **Whether `4-panstep` fires in the browser on the field-guarantee retirement.** It fires on
  **neither** browser race on disk (0 frames ≥ 1280 px on both, maxima 296 px and 742 px). The
  headless arm produces 1377.5 px on mountainstreet and 1207.9 px on seatrack — neither of which is a
  gate track, and neither of which has a browser dump. **The run that settles it:**
  `node scripts/viewer-invariants.mjs --tracks=mountainstreet,seatrack --seeds=1-40 --dump --json=…`
  then `node scripts/diag/judder-census.mjs --json=…`.
- **Whether the ×14.8 browser retirement generalises.** It is **one event, in one race, on one track,
  at one seed, on the `his` arm.** Per `feedback_screen_at_n30_first` every number carries its own N
  and this one is N=1. It is enough to falsify "the pan channel needs no marker"; it is not enough to
  set a bound.
- **Whether the field retirement's zoom step is acceptable to the owner.** Both reports that measured
  it explicitly declined the verdict.
- **The frame-clock dependence of both bounds.** `RACE-JUDDER-1` measured **28.6% of real-clock frames
  delivered at two vsyncs**, and `SEED-117-SETTLE-1.md:102–105` records the same zoom arriving in one
  frame at one clock and four at another. On a doubled frame the picture legitimately moves twice as
  far. **Both channels must divide by `dt`, or run only on the virtual clock.** I did not measure how
  much that changes either distribution.
- **`_companyIsHome`'s behaviour at a real finish.** The shared driver's loop ends at the last
  crossing, so the finish-mode branch had no frames to fire in on any of the ten headless races. Its
  marker gap (§3.7) is established from the source, not from a measurement of the event.
- **Whether the medians hold under `--arm=his` across ten tracks.** The two browser races are both
  `his`; the ten headless races are all shipped defaults. The two sets agree on the median to within
  40%, which is suggestive and is not a sweep.
