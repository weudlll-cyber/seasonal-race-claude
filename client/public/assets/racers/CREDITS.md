# Racer sprite sheets — provenance and the one-off crop they went through

**Owns:** where these files came from, and **what they were before the 2026-06-03 tight-crop pass**.
The LIVE frame geometry of every sheet is not here — it is in the racer-type registry
(`client/src/modules/racer-types/*RacerType.js`), which is its one home. **Nothing below restates a
live value.**

---

## The tight-crop pass, 2026-06-03 (`11093ff`)

Twelve sheets were flagged for a bounding-box fill under 50% and cropped **in place**: each frame
extracted, padded, cropped to the union body box plus 15 px, squared, centred, Lanczos3-resized, and
stitched back into a new horizontal sheet. Mask files were cropped with identical parameters. Five
types had their `displaySize` raised proportionally afterwards, because the crop pushed
`cropSize > frameHeight` and would otherwise have shrunk the drawn body.

**The tool that did it — `scripts/crop-sprite-sheets.mjs` — was DELETED on 2026-09-03
(DROP-CROP-SCRIPT-1).** It had done its work; what it could still do was destructive only. It
overwrote its inputs, and its recorded pre-crop geometry no longer matched the files on disk, so
running it again would have sliced each sheet at the wrong offsets and written the result back over
the artwork — which is exactly what happened, by accident, on 2026-09-03. It is recoverable in full
at the annotated tag **`archive/crop-sprite-sheets`**.

### What each sheet held BEFORE the crop

**This table is the only record of these numbers.** It is here, beside the files, because that is
where somebody wondering why a sheet is 129 px wide will be looking. It is a HISTORICAL record: the
"after" column is included only so the pair is legible, and the registry — not this file — is what
the product reads.

| sheet | frame BEFORE | frame after | frames | masks cropped with it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `horse-trot.png` | 128×128 | 150×150 | 8 | — |
| `giraffe-walk.png` | 128×128 | 129×129 | 8 | — |
| `snake-crawl.png` | 128×128 | 155×155 | 8 | — |
| `rocket-fly.png` | 128×128 | 151×151 | 8 | — |
| `motorbike-walk.png` | 128×128 | 150×150 | 8 | `motorbike-walk-mask.png` |
| `luge-slide.png` | 64×64 | 128×128 | 16 | — |
| `beetle.png` | 128×128 | 128×128 | 8 | — |
| `boarder-sprite.png` | 128×128 | 128×128 | 12 | — |
| `koi-swim.png` | 565×565 | 256×256 | 16 | `koi-mask-kohaku.png`, `koi-mask-ogon.png`, `koi-mask-sanke.png`, `koi-mask-showa.png` |
| `turtle-swim.png` | 128×128 | 128×128 | 16 | `turtle-mask-plates.png`, `turtle-mask-borders.png` |
| `dolphin-swim.png` | 256×256 | 256×256 | 16 | `dolphin-mask-belly.png` |
| `snowmobile.png` | 192×192 | 148×148 | 16 | — |

**Four of the twelve came out the size they went in** — beetle, boarder, turtle and dolphin. Their
bodies were upscaled inside an unchanged frame, which is why the crop is invisible in their geometry
and visible in everyone else's.

**How the "after" column was established, rather than copied:** the deleted tool's own arithmetic —
`cropSize = max(bodyWidth, bodyHeight) + 30`, then `targetSize = 128` below 128, `cropSize` up to 256,
`256` above — was run over the pre-crop geometry it recorded and compared with each PNG's IHDR header.
**Twelve of twelve predicted the frame size the file actually has.** That is the proof the sheets in
this directory are that pass's output, and therefore that deleting the tool loses no capability.
